10 POLICY

ÉIRE LÁIDIR

Managed Migration, Residency & Employment Policy

Standalone policy draft for review — to be considered for inclusion in the main Éire Láidir policy document.

POLICY PRINCIPLE

Come legally. Contribute. Become self-sufficient. Respect Irish law.

Éire Láidir proposes a managed migration system based on lawful entry, contribution, self-sufficiency, strong employer responsibility and firm enforcement. The objective is not to close Ireland to legitimate migration, but to ensure that migration is controlled, sustainable and aligned with the national interest.

1. Social Housing — Citizens First

  • Social housing should prioritise Irish citizens and households with a genuine long-term connection to Ireland, subject to constitutional, EU and international legal obligations.
  • Eligibility rules should be tightened so that access is based on lawful residency, habitual residency, contribution and genuine housing need.
  • Government should review existing allocations and eligibility arrangements to ensure that the social housing system is primarily serving citizens and long-term qualifying residents.

2. Welfare & State Support — Contribution-Based Access

  • Access to social welfare should be linked as far as legally possible to lawful residence, habitual residence, employment contribution and genuine need.
  • The State should review welfare eligibility for non-citizens and strengthen contribution and residency requirements while respecting EU free-movement rights and international obligations.
  • The objective is to ensure that Ireland's welfare system remains sustainable and is not a primary incentive for migration.

3. International Protection & Return

  • Ireland should maintain a firm and efficient international-protection process, with decisions made as quickly as practicable.
  • Where a person has no legal basis to remain, the State should pursue lawful return arrangements promptly and effectively.
  • Government should expand voluntary-return supports, including practical assistance and, where appropriate, travel arrangements, while maintaining lawful enforcement procedures.
  • The policy objective is a functioning system where protection is provided to those entitled to it, while unsuccessful claims do not result in indefinite residence.

4. NGO & State Funding Review

  • All State funding provided to organisations working in migration, accommodation, integration or international protection should be subject to transparent contracts, measurable outcomes and independent value-for-money review.
  • Funding should not automatically continue simply because an organisation operates in the sector.
  • Where services are required, Government should consider direct public provision or competitive procurement where this delivers better value and accountability.

5. Accommodation Support for Temporary Migrants

  • For temporary employment migrants, accommodation should be primarily an employer responsibility where the employment arrangement creates a clear housing requirement.
  • Where limited State accommodation support is legally available, it should be targeted, time-limited and linked to lawful status and employment.
  • Employers recruiting overseas workers should demonstrate that suitable accommodation arrangements are available before workers arrive.

6. Non-EEA Employment Visa — Employer Sponsored

  • Non-EEA employment visas should operate on a stronger employer-sponsored model.
  • The employer should carry the principal administrative and financial responsibility for the application, including required fees and documentation.
  • Employers should provide substantial evidence demonstrating why overseas recruitment is necessary and why suitable candidates cannot reasonably be sourced in Ireland or the wider EEA, where such tests are legally permissible.

7. Employer-Tied Work Visa

  • Where legally permissible, employment permission should be tied to the sponsoring employer and approved occupation.
  • If employment ends, the worker should have a defined and limited period to secure lawful alternative employment or leave the State, subject to Irish and EU law.
  • This would make employers accountable for the workers they recruit and reduce abuse of employment-permit arrangements.

8. Employer-Funded Private Healthcare

  • Employers sponsoring temporary non-EEA workers should be required to provide comprehensive private health insurance for the duration of the employment permission, where legally permissible.
  • The objective is to reduce avoidable pressure on publicly funded services while ensuring workers have appropriate access to healthcare.

9. Employer-Funded Accommodation Supplement

  • Where a low-paid sponsored worker's wages are insufficient to cover reasonable accommodation costs, the sponsoring employer should carry an additional accommodation obligation.
  • This could operate through employer-provided accommodation or a regulated accommodation allowance.
  • The intention is to prevent low-wage recruitment models from shifting accommodation costs onto the State.

10. Qualification & Credential Verification

  • All qualifications relied upon for employment permits should be independently verified where required.
  • Verification costs should be borne by the sponsoring employer rather than the Irish taxpayer.
  • False qualifications or fraudulent documentation should result in refusal, cancellation of permission and appropriate legal action.

11. Full Criminal-Record Screening

  • Employment visa applicants should undergo comprehensive criminal-record and identity checks to the fullest extent legally and practically possible.
  • Serious criminal convictions should be assessed as part of the immigration and employment-permission process, subject to Irish law, proportionality and applicable rights.
  • Identity verification should be robust and completed before employment begins.

12. Make Recruitment Responsible — Not Closed

  • Éire Láidir would not advocate closing Ireland to legitimate overseas workers where there are genuine skills shortages.
  • Instead, the system should make recruitment responsible: employers should carry the real financial, administrative and compliance costs associated with overseas recruitment.
  • The aim is to encourage employers to recruit and train people already living in Ireland or the EEA where suitable candidates are available.

13. Employer Compliance Penalties

  • Employers who breach employment-permit, wage, accommodation, healthcare or immigration rules should face meaningful penalties.
  • Repeated or serious breaches should result in loss of sponsorship privileges, financial penalties and prosecution where appropriate.
  • Workers should not be punished for an employer's misconduct where they have acted lawfully.

14. A Contribution-Based Immigration Model

  • Ireland should move toward an immigration model in which legal migration is sustainable, economically justified and based on clear rules.
  • Temporary workers should understand from the outset the conditions attached to their permission, including employment status, accommodation arrangements and the consequences of ending employment.
  • People who contribute, work legally and respect Irish law should have a clear and predictable route through the immigration system, consistent with Irish, EU and international law.

EXPECTED OUTCOMES

  • Greater employer responsibility for overseas recruitment.
  • Less incentive for employers to rely on overseas recruitment where suitable local or EEA workers are available.
  • Stronger screening and verification of temporary migrants.
  • Reduced pressure on publicly funded accommodation, welfare and healthcare where employer responsibility can lawfully apply.
  • A faster, clearer and more enforceable immigration system.
  • A migration system designed around contribution, self-sufficiency and the national interest.

LEGAL SAFEGUARD

This is a political policy proposal for review. Any final implementation would need to comply with the Constitution, EU free-movement and employment law, international protection obligations, human-rights law and existing Irish legislation. Where EU or international rules limit national discretion, Éire Láidir would seek lawful reform, negotiated changes or stronger national discretion rather than assuming that existing rules can simply be ignored.
